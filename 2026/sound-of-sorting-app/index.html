Sound of Sorting - The Mobile App: "Audibilization" and Visualization of Sorting Algorithms on your Phone and Tablet

Posted on 2026-04-26 by Timo Bingmann at Permlink.

The Sound of Sorting visualizes 32 classic sorting algorithms with live audio and motion. Every comparison rings a note; every swap redraws a bar. Watch - and listen to - how each algorithm pulls an array from chaos into order.

Originally released in 2013 as a C++ demo for university algorithms classes, Sound of Sorting has been viewed tens of millions of times on YouTube. This is the mobile app version: native, fast, and rebuilt for touch.

Who It's For

  • CS students learning how sorts actually compare in practice
  • Instructors looking for a lecture demo that's easier to launch than explaining the gcc source code live
  • Engineers who want to rewatch a Quick Sort partition and finally see why it's fast
  • Anyone who finds audio-visual algorithm traces calming to watch

Features

  • 32 sorting algorithms, from familiar (Bubble, Selection, Insertion) to obscure (Cycle Sort, Smooth Sort, Block Merge Sort)
  • Real-time audio: every array access plays a tone whose pitch matches the value being read
  • Colored bars: access highlights, permanent marks, sorted regions - all in live animation
  • Adjustable array size (8 to 2048 elements) and animation speed
  • Step mode: advance one comparison at a time
  • Infinite Play: play a never-ending parade of random sorts
  • Live stats: comparison count and access count update as the sort runs
  • Runs entirely on-device: no network, no analytics, no accounts

About the Project

Sound of Sorting began in 2013 as a cross-platform C++ demo built with wxWidgets and SDL, written for an undergraduate algorithms course at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology. The YouTube video went viral and the demo has been used in lectures, tutorials, and bedtime watching ever since.

The 2026 app is a separate native rewrite for iOS and Android - same idea, redesigned for touch and individual exploration with algorithm descriptions.

The original 2013 demo, with downloadable source archives, Win32 and Linux binaries, the cheat-sheet PDF, and the full ChangeLog, lives on at panthema.net/2013/sound-of-sorting/. Its sources remain available under the GNU GPL v3 at github.com/bingmann/sound-of-sorting.
